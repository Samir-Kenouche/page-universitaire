Fundamental Integral Equations of Electron Delocalization

From the pair density to the delocalization index: definitions, sum rules and their physical meaning.

Pair Density and Exchange-Correlation Hole

Conventions. N is the number of electrons, ρ(𝐫) the one-electron density, 𝒜 and ℬ atomic basins bounded by zero-flux surfaces of ∇ρ (QTAIM). All quantities are spin-summed.

Space-spin coordinates

Each electron is labeled by a space-spin coordinate x=(𝐫,σ), where 𝐫∈ℝ3 is its position and σ∈{↑,↓} its spin.

∫dxf(x)=∑σ∫d𝐫f(𝐫,σ)=∫d𝐫[f(𝐫,↑)+f(𝐫,↓)]

Physical meaning. An electron is described by where it is (𝐫) and by its spin (↑ or ↓). Integrating over x therefore means integrating over position and summing over the two possible spin values. This is the meaning of the integrals dx2⋯dxN and of the spin sums in the definitions below. Spin-summed quantities such as ρ(𝐫1) and ρ2(𝐫1,𝐫2) depend on positions only.

Pair density

ρ2(𝐫1,𝐫2)=N(N−1)∑σ1,σ2∫|Ψ|2dx3⋯dxN ∬ρ2(𝐫1,𝐫2)d𝐫1d𝐫2=N(N−1)

Physical meaning. The pair density is the joint probability density of finding one electron at 𝐫1 and another at 𝐫2 at the same time. Its integral counts the N(N−1) ordered pairs of electrons. It is the central object from which every delocalization measure is built.

Exchange-correlation hole

ρ2(𝐫1,𝐫2)=ρ(𝐫1)[ρ(𝐫2)+hxc(𝐫1,𝐫2)]

Physical meaning. If electrons were independent, the pair density would be the plain product ρ(𝐫1)ρ(𝐫2). The hole hxc(𝐫1,𝐫2) is the correction: the density change around an electron placed at 𝐫1. It combines the Pauli exclusion of same-spin electrons (exchange, or Fermi hole) and Coulomb repulsion (correlation).

Hole sum rule

∫hxc(𝐫1,𝐫2)d𝐫2=−1

Physical meaning. The hole carries exactly one unit of positive charge: an electron expels one electron's worth of density from its surroundings. This is the removal of self-interaction, and it is what makes the hole integrals below add up to whole electrons.

Exchange-correlation pair density

γxc(𝐫1,𝐫2)=ρ2(𝐫1,𝐫2)−ρ(𝐫1)ρ(𝐫2)=ρ(𝐫1)hxc(𝐫1,𝐫2) ∬γxc(𝐫1,𝐫2)d𝐫1d𝐫2=−N

Physical meaning. It measures how far electron positions are correlated beyond the independent-particle picture. It is symmetric in its two arguments and negative on average, since electrons avoid each other. Integrating it over atomic basins is how electron sharing is quantified.

Integration over a single variable

ρ(𝐫1)=N∑σ1∫|Ψ|2dx2⋯dxN

Physical meaning. Integrating |Ψ|2 over all electrons but one gives the probability density of finding an electron at 𝐫1. The factor N accounts for any of the identical electrons being the one observed.

∫ρ2(𝐫1,𝐫2)d𝐫2=(N−1)ρ(𝐫1),∫ρ2(𝐫1,𝐫2)d𝐫1=(N−1)ρ(𝐫2)

Physical meaning. With one electron fixed at 𝐫1 (probability density ρ(𝐫1)), the other N−1 electrons are found somewhere in space with certainty. Integrating the pair density over the second position therefore counts them all. The pair density thus contains the one-electron density: ρ(𝐫1)=1N−1∫ρ2(𝐫1,𝐫2)d𝐫2. Inserting the hole factorization gives ρ(𝐫1)[N−1]: the density integrates to N and the hole removes one electron.

∫γxc(𝐫1,𝐫2)d𝐫2=−ρ(𝐫1),∫γxc(𝐫1,𝐫2)d𝐫1=−ρ(𝐫2)

Physical meaning. This is the local form of the hole sum rule. Since γxc=ρ(𝐫1)hxc and the hole integrates to −1, the exchange-correlation pair density removes, at every point 𝐫1, a charge equal to the local density. Integrating once more over 𝐫1 returns the global value −N.

One-electron reduced density matrix

γ(𝐫1,𝐫2)=∑iniϕi(𝐫1)ϕi∗(𝐫2) ρ(𝐫)=γ(𝐫,𝐫)=∑ini|ϕi(𝐫)|2,∫γ(𝐫,𝐫)d𝐫=∑ini=N

Physical meaning. The one-electron reduced density matrix γ(𝐫1,𝐫2) generalizes the density: its diagonal γ(𝐫,𝐫) is ρ(𝐫), while its off-diagonal elements measure the quantum coherence between two positions, the information that makes electrons exchange. It is written with the natural orbitals ϕi, its eigenfunctions, and their occupation numbers ni, which satisfy 0≤ni≤2 in the spin-summed convention and sum to N. For a closed-shell single determinant, ni=2 for occupied orbitals and 0 for the others; correlation produces fractional values. The squared modulus |γ(𝐫1,𝐫2)|2 is the building block of the ALM approximation of the pair density given below.

Integrals over Atomic Basins

Basin population

N(𝒜)=∫𝒜ρ(𝐫)d𝐫,∑𝒜N(𝒜)=N

Physical meaning. The average number of electrons in basin 𝒜. Basins tile space without overlap, so their populations sum to N. The atomic charge follows as Z𝒜−N(𝒜).

Localization index

λ(𝒜)=−∫𝒜d𝐫1∫𝒜d𝐫2γxc(𝐫1,𝐫2)

Physical meaning. The number of electrons that stay within basin 𝒜: the part of N(𝒜) that is not exchanged with other basins. It equals N(𝒜) for an isolated atom or a perfectly closed shell, as in an ideal ionic limit.

Delocalization index

δ(𝒜,ℬ)=−2∫𝒜d𝐫1∫ℬd𝐫2γxc(𝐫1,𝐫2)

Physical meaning. The number of electron pairs shared between basins 𝒜 and ℬ, hence a bond-order measure rooted in the pair density. Within Hartree-Fock it is close to 1 for a single covalent bond and close to 2 for a double bond, and it is small for ionic or non-bonded pairs. The Dual Delocalization Descriptor (DDD) is the response of this quantity to a change in N.

One variable over a basin, the other over all space

∫𝒜d𝐫1∫ρ2(𝐫1,𝐫2)d𝐫2=(N−1)N(𝒜),∫𝒜d𝐫1∫γxc(𝐫1,𝐫2)d𝐫2=−N(𝒜)

Physical meaning. Restricting the first position to basin 𝒜 and leaving the second free, each of the N(𝒜) electrons of 𝒜 has N−1 partners, and the hole attached to them totals −N(𝒜). Splitting the free integral over all basins ℬ shows where that hole goes: −N(𝒜)=−λ(𝒜)−12∑ℬ≠𝒜δ(𝒜,ℬ). The part inside 𝒜 is the localization, the parts in other basins are the delocalization. This is the origin of the sum rule below.

∫𝒜d𝐫1∫ℬd𝐫2ρ2(𝐫1,𝐫2)=N(𝒜)N(ℬ)−12δ(𝒜,ℬ),∫𝒜d𝐫1∫𝒜d𝐫2ρ2(𝐫1,𝐫2)=N(𝒜)2−λ(𝒜)

Physical meaning. These are the average numbers of ordered electron pairs with one electron in each basin. For two different basins, the independent-electron product N(𝒜)N(ℬ) is reduced by half the delocalization index: sharing electrons makes the two populations anticorrelated. Within one basin, the number of pairs is N(𝒜)2 reduced by the localization index.

Electron-counting sum rule

N(𝒜)=λ(𝒜)+12∑ℬ≠𝒜δ(𝒜,ℬ) N=∑𝒜λ(𝒜)+12∑𝒜∑ℬ≠𝒜δ(𝒜,ℬ)

Physical meaning. Every electron is either localized in one basin or shared between two. The factor 12 avoids counting each pair twice. This identity follows directly from the hole sum rule and is the basis for reading δ as electrons.

Population fluctuations

σ2(N𝒜)=⟨N𝒜2⟩−⟨N𝒜⟩2=N(𝒜)−λ(𝒜)=12∑ℬ≠𝒜δ(𝒜,ℬ) cov(N𝒜,Nℬ)=∫𝒜d𝐫1∫ℬd𝐫2γxc(𝐫1,𝐫2)=−12δ(𝒜,ℬ)

Physical meaning. Delocalization is the quantum fluctuation of a basin's electron count: the variance of N𝒜 grows with the electrons it shares. The negative covariance shows that when basin 𝒜 gains an electron in an instantaneous configuration, basin ℬ tends to lose one.

Orbital Formulas

Atomic overlap matrix and the single-determinant limit

Sij(𝒜)=∫𝒜ϕi∗(𝐫)ϕj(𝐫)d𝐫,∑𝒜Sij(𝒜)=δij δHF(𝒜,ℬ)=4∑i,jSij(𝒜)Sji(ℬ)

Physical meaning. Sij(𝒜) is the overlap of orbitals ϕi and ϕj restricted to one basin: how much two orbitals mix inside an atom. The atomic matrices sum to the identity, so orbitals stay orthonormal over all space. For a closed-shell single determinant the double integral collapses to this sum, taken over the N/2 doubly occupied spatial orbitals.

ALM approximation for correlated wave functions

γxcALM(𝐫1,𝐫2)=−12|γ(𝐫1,𝐫2)|2,γ(𝐫1,𝐫2)=∑iniϕi(𝐫1)ϕi∗(𝐫2) δALM(𝒜,ℬ)=∑i,jninjSij(𝒜)Sji(ℬ)

Physical meaning. Without the exact pair density, the Ángyán-Loos-Mayer scheme rebuilds it from the one-electron reduced density matrix γ(𝐫1,𝐫2), written with natural orbitals ϕi and occupations ni. Fractional occupations carry correlation effects into the delocalization index. With ni=2 or 0 it reduces to the Hartree-Fock formula above. Because δ(𝒜,ℬ) is bilinear in the occupations, it is differentiable with respect to N, which is what the DDD exploits.
