1. Theoretical Framework & Mathematical Formalism

The Dual Delocalization Descriptor (DDD) is a theoretical descriptor developed by Prof. Samir Kenouche and published in Physical Chemistry Chemical Physics (Phys. Chem. Chem. Phys., 2026, 28(31), 19133–19145). Derived from an exact analytical response theory of electron delocalization to variations in the total number of electrons, its decisive advantage lies in explicit analytical formulas directly implementable in quantum chemistry software. In particular, the DDD relies solely on the ground-state wave function of the neutral species, completely bypassing the need for calculations on ionic states.

A. ALM Scheme Approximation of the Delocalization Index

Within the ALM (Ángyán–Loos–Mayer) scheme, the exchange-correlation pair density is reconstructed as:

γxcALM(𝐫1,𝐫2)=−12|γ(𝐫1,𝐫2)|2

Expanding the 1-electron reduced density matrix (1-RDM) in terms of natural orbitals ϕi and their occupation numbers ni:

γ(𝐫1,𝐫2)=∑iniϕi(𝐫1)ϕi∗(𝐫2)

Introducing a second summation index j, the squared magnitude of the 1-RDM expands as:

|γ(𝐫1,𝐫2)|2=∑i,jninjϕi(𝐫1)ϕj∗(𝐫1)ϕi∗(𝐫2)ϕj(𝐫2)

Substituting this orbital expansion into the ALM pair density formulation yields the explicit expression:

γxcALM(𝐫1,𝐫2)=−12∑i,jninjϕi(𝐫1)ϕj∗(𝐫1)ϕi∗(𝐫2)ϕj(𝐫2)

Substituting this explicit pair density into the definition of the QTAIM delocalization index δ(𝒜,ℬ) between atomic basins 𝒜 and ℬ:

δALM(𝒜,ℬ)=−2∫𝒜d𝐫1∫ℬd𝐫2γxcALM(𝐫1,𝐫2)=∑i,jninjSij(𝒜)Sji(ℬ)

where Sij(𝒜)=∫𝒜ϕi∗(𝐫)ϕj(𝐫)d𝐫 denotes the basin-restricted atomic overlap matrix (AOM) element.

B. Dual Response & Directional Derivatives

To quantify the response of electron delocalization to changes in total electron number N, the DDD evaluates the left- and right-hand directional limits:

(∂δ(𝒜,ℬ)∂N)−=limΔN→0−δ(𝒜,ℬ;N0+ΔN)−δ(𝒜,ℬ;N0)ΔN (∂δ(𝒜,ℬ)∂N)+=limΔN→0+δ(𝒜,ℬ;N0+ΔN)−δ(𝒜,ℬ;N0)ΔN

The Dual Delocalization Descriptor is algebraically defined as the difference between right- and left-hand responses:

Δδ(𝒜,ℬ)=(∂δ(𝒜,ℬ)∂N)+−(∂δ(𝒜,ℬ)∂N)−

C. Chemical Utility & Physical Interpretation

Formulated through first-order and second-order responses of the interatomic delocalization index to variations in total electron number N, the DDD serves as a bond-resolved probe of electron redistribution during chemical perturbations.

By capturing how interatomic electron sharing dynamically responds upon electron addition or removal, it reflects the relative interplay between nucleophilic (electron-attachment) and electrophilic (electron-removal) channels:

  • Positive values (Δδ(𝒜,ℬ)>0): Indicate a net enhancement in interatomic electron sharing between the two atomic basins.
  • Negative values (Δδ(𝒜,ℬ)<0): Indicate a net depletion in electron sharing between the two atomic basins.
Complete Derivations & Proofs: For full mathematical derivations, the analytical formulas as well as chemical and physical applications, please refer to the foundational paper:
S. Kenouche, Phys. Chem. Chem. Phys., 2026, 28(31), 19133–19145 (DOI: 10.1039/D6CP01777K).

2. International Software Integration in Multiwfn v2026.10.1

Through collaboration with Prof. Tian Lu (Beijing Kein Research Center for Natural Sciences), the DDD has been officially implemented into the Conceptual DFT (Density Functional Theory) module of Multiwfn, the most widely used quantum chemistry post-processing program globally.

"The dual delocalization descriptor (DDD) proposed in Phys. Chem. Chem. Phys., 28, 19133 (2026) by Samir Kenouche has been implemented in conceptual DFT module. This theory analytically quantifies responses of the interatomic delocalization index (DI) to variations in the total electron number. See Section 3.25.6 of manual for introduction and Section 4.22.6 for example."
